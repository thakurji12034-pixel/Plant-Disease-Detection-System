📖 Understanding Plant Diseases

A plant disease is defined as any abnormal symptom or biological condition that essentially alters the appearance, growth, or physiological function of a plant. Just like humans, plants suffer from ailments that can stunt their growth, discolor their leaves, or even lead to their death.

In the agricultural ecosystem, diseases are a major barrier to food security. They dramatically reduce crop yield and quality. Early detection and identification are the most critical steps to isolate the disease, prevent it from spreading, and apply the correct treatments in time.

🔬 Deep Learning Solution

This system harnesses the power of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN - MobileNetV2). By feeding an image of a leaf into the AI model, the system analyzes its visual patterns—like spots, discoloration, and textures—to accurately classify its health status.

⚡ Why Early Detection?

Identifying a disease precisely when symptoms first appear helps farmers take immediate restorative actions. It minimizes the need for excessive harmful pesticides, reduces financial loss, and significantly boosts overall crop productivity.

⚠️ Why Do Plant Diseases Happen?

Plant diseases occur when a susceptible host plant, a virulent pathogen, and favorable environmental conditions coincide. This relationship is often referred to as the Disease Triangle.

🦠 Common Pathogens

  • Fungi: The leading cause of plant diseases. They manifest as mold, rust, mildew, or spots and thrive in damp, highly humid environments.
  • Bacteria: These microscopic organisms enter plants through wounds or natural openings, leading to blights, wilts, or soft rots.
  • Viruses: Often transmitted by insects like aphids, viruses alter plant genetics, causing mosaic patterns, stunted growth, and distorted leaves.

🌩️ Environmental Stress

Non-infectious diseases (disorders) are caused by hostile environmental or physical conditions rather than living organisms.

  • Nutrient deficiencies or toxicities in the soil.
  • Extreme temperature fluctuations (frost or heatwaves).
  • Improper watering habits (drought stress or waterlogging).
  • Poor soil structure and aeration.
